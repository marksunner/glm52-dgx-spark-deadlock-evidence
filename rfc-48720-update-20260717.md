# RFC #48720 results update — 2026-07-17, final numbers 2026-07-18 (for posting by Mark/Tars)

## TL;DR
The campaign's two remaining mysteries are resolved, with receipts:

1. **The sub-boundary freeze class (all 8 of this week's "climb stalls," R2a
   — the first enforce-round attempt, voided mid-climb; bundle
   R2a-void-TypeB-20260716T144754Z — and the "cold 200K prefill never
   completes" finding) was a single platform
   bug**: the flashinfer `sparse_mla_sm120` attention kernels livelock on
   GB10/sm_121 — cuda-gdb on a live wedge shows one resident block of
   `sparse_mla_prefill_kernel<…, PrefillColdParams>` spinning at
   `SYNCS.PHASECHK.TRANS64.TRYWAIT` (mbarrier expect-tx race), GPU at 96 %
   util / ~18 W / 0 % memory traffic, all host threads piled into
   cuLaunchKernel behind the jammed queue. Probability scales with
   cold-prefill tile count (warm/prefix-cached prefills never wedge — which
   is why R1 succeeded on a warm cluster; R1 = the observe-mode boundary
   round of 2026-07-16, bundle R1-capture-20260716T131548Z). Full dossier:
   the companion NVIDIA-facing write-up published alongside this update;
   the raw cuda-gdb capture is in `evidence-pack/cuda-gdb/`.
   Workaround: route the main model to the portable Triton sparse-MLA
   implementations (`--attention-backend FLASHMLA_SPARSE` + the sm12x Triton
   drop-ins; the drop-ins' kernels come verbatim from the deepseek_v4 path
   of jasl's vLLM fork — credit @jasl, whose Triton sparse-MLA
   implementations are the substrate of this entire workaround). Zero
   wedges since.

2. **Mechanism #1 (rank-divergent drafter gating at the context ceiling,
   §4/§5 of this RFC — §4 is the mechanism taxonomy, §5 the
   prevention-not-recovery platform facts) did not reproduce on the Triton
   attention stack.**
   With the served ceiling lowered to 120K (the mechanism, gate jurisdiction,
   and boundary payload are all ceiling-relative), we ran 30 boundary
   sessions under gate=observe and 30 under gate=enforce — every session
   decoding through seq 119,997–120,000. The drafter-gate jurisdiction fired
   on all four ranks in lockstep (20 `GLM52_DRAFTER skip` events per rank,
   identical counts and timing), with ZERO gate divergence, ZERO fingerprint
   mismatches, and ZERO wedges across all 60 sessions. Our read, stated as
   what it is: non-reproduction on the Triton route is CONSISTENT WITH the
   hypothesis that mechanism #1's seed — nondeterministic sm12x
   attention/MoE kernels producing bit-divergent logits, hence
   rank-divergent rejection counts at the boundary — went away with the
   flashinfer kernels. It is not proof of causation: no direct bit-level
   determinism comparison of the two stacks was performed.
   The 2026-07-16 12:59Z R1 capture (200K ceiling, flashinfer stack, gate
   disagreement 123/122/122/122 followed by the RAS-frozen wedge) remains
   the definitive live specimen of the mechanism.

## 2026-07-18 addendum — 200K restored on the Triton route; completed statistics

1. **The full advertised 200K ceiling is restored.** On 2026-07-18 we ran a
   single probe at the ORIGINAL 200K config (max_model_len 200000,
   kv_cache_memory_bytes 10.95e9, everything else pinned incl. the Triton
   attention route and gate=enforce): a cold staged climb of 64 prefix-cache
   stages to 199,872 tokens completed with ZERO stalls (RAS op counts equal,
   spread 0 at every sample; cold-delta prefill ~680 tok/s), followed by a
   boundary session decoding through seq 199,997–200,000 (prompt 199,872 +
   128 generated = exactly 200,000; finish=length, coherent output,
   **~26 tok/s decode at the 200K boundary**). Server-side readout, all four
   ranks: drafter-gate jurisdiction exercised (`GLM52_DRAFTER skip` 3/rank,
   lockstep), gate DIVERGENCE/FORCED/ENFORCED = 0, fingerprint MISMATCH = 0.
   This is the first cold-route 200K completion in the campaign (on the
   flashinfer stack, every cold ≥60K attempt wedged, n=8, and cold 200K never
   completed inside 1800 s). **The 120K figure in the section below is
   therefore a headroom CHOICE (standing posture), not a platform limit.**
   Scope, precisely: 200K is validated by this single full probe — one
   launch, one staged climb, one boundary session — and the production
   standing posture remains 120K by choice; operators wanting 200K should
   replicate the probe config given at the bottom of this update.
2. **Completed statistics: zero wedges across 560+ ceiling sessions on the
   Triton route**, including **500 consecutive sessions under gate=enforce**
   (the 30 R2-verdict sessions + a 470-session overnight statistics run,
   every session decoding through seq 119,997–120,000, finish=length every
   time). Availability and quality are separate metrics here: availability
   is wedges 0/560+, while quality carries one disclosed sampling-artifact
   outlier (the footnote below). Client-side receipts for the overnight window: the soak driver log
   (staged climb + soak-30 `SOAK DONE rc=0`, then 470/470 `ok` with a clean
   terminator), the periodic gate self-report milestones observed in the rank
   docker logs (`GLM52_GATE OK … steps=20000 div=0 forced=0`), and ~15 h
   container uptime across the window (standing-posture launch 13:46Z →
   wave-day teardown 04:54Z, journaled) with the external watchdog silent
   (zero auto-restarts). Honesty footnote: one session (470/470) showed an
   incoherent tail — a temp-1.2 sampling artifact, not a stall (11 s vs the
   4–5 s norm, single instance, journaled).
3. **Throughput (the workaround's cost, measured):** warm decode at the 120K
   boundary runs **~25–27 tok/s** (120-token boundary sessions completing in
   4–5 s wall, n=500), and **~26 tok/s at the 200K boundary** — at or above
   the ~23 tok/s historic baseline of the flashinfer stack. The Triton route
   costs nothing measurable in decode throughput on this platform.

## Gate (consensus drafter-gate fix) status
- **Safety at the ceiling: proven, now at n=500.** 500 consecutive enforce
  sessions at seq 119,997–120,000 (30 R2-verdict + 470-session statistics
  run) plus one enforce session at seq 199,997–200,000 (the original 200K
  geometry): no crash, no OOB, no false enforcement, engine steps lockstep
  across ranks throughout. Quality basis, stated exactly: byte-identical
  smoke determinism checks across boots, spot-checked coherent boundary
  completions, and one disclosed sampling-artifact outlier; a formal
  quality evaluation was out of scope.
- **Efficacy: not exercised** — no divergence occurred to enforce against on
  the current stack. The gate stays deployed armed (enforce) as insurance;
  it is the correct defense-in-depth for any stack where per-rank
  nondeterminism can reappear (kernel upgrades, autotune changes).

## Corrections to earlier sections
- §3/L3 (this RFC's platform-bug ledger entry for the GB10 0x51 UMA memdesc
  leak): still real and independent, but it was NOT
  the cause of this week's climb stalls (those froze with 5–7 GB free after
  we lowered the served ceiling; RoCE and driver also exonerated with
  receipts).
- The "marlin/UMA-ceiling spin" interpretation of the 07-17 morning stalls
  is retracted: deeper native stacks show marlin was a bystander at a full
  launch queue; the spinning device kernel was sparse-MLA (cuda-gdb receipt).
- "GMU 0.91 reserves ~113 GB" is a wrong attribution: with
  `kv_cache_memory_bytes` set, vLLM skips memory profiling and
  `gpu_memory_utilization`'s only consumer is a boot-time free-memory
  validation (worker/utils.py request_memory). The knob allocates nothing.
- Platform note for vLLM: on capability-12 devices the MLA-sparse candidate
  list is [TRITON_MLA, FLASHINFER_MLA_SPARSE_SM120] only; when the flashinfer
  sm120 kernels are the sole viable pick they are selected without any
  fallback. Given the livelock above, consider exposing/preferring a Triton
  sparse path (or at least a selector escape hatch) on sm12x until the
  kernels are fixed.
